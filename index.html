I am a doctoral researcher at the School of Social Sciences and Humanities, University of Wolverhampton, examining how technology, mobility, and media infrastructures shape gendered identities and social stratification in contemporary transnational circuits.

My doctoral research—“Scripting the Settler Dream: Instagram Influencers, Generative AI, and the Negotiation of Malayali Masculinities in UK Adult Social Care”—draws on multi-sited field ethnography with Malayali migrant care workers to examine masculinity, settler aspiration, and labour precarity in Britain's post-Brexit care economy.

A closely related strand investigates how migrant workers leverage conversational AI interfaces, such as ChatGPT and Google Gemini, to negotiate the linguistic, emotional, and regulatory demands of professional adult social care, developing the novel conceptual paradigm of algorithmic acculturation. Alongside my qualitative ethnography, I bring statistical-methods and computational training, integrating interpretive analysis with social media scraping and discourse analysis.

My research has been recognized and supported by competitive fellowships and awards from the Swedish Research Council (Vetenskapsrådet), the Swedish Collegium for Advanced Study (SCAS), the European Sociological Association (ESA), ARL Leibniz Association / RSA, and SOAS, University of London. I have published in leading peer-reviewed venues including the International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction, Economic and Political Weekly, and the Journal of Ethnic and Migration Studies (forthcoming).
